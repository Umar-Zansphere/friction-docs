How Friction Works

Pause
A short delay before an app opens gives you time to decide.
Strict mode
Locks the slide button until an intention is selected.
Throttle
Slow down an app's feed so it's less compelling to scroll.
Hard block
Completely bars access during a set time window.

Frequently Asked Questions

Friction adds intentional speed bumps before you open apps you've identified as distracting. Instead of blocking apps outright, it gives you a moment to decide if opening them is intentional — a pause screen, a question about why you're opening the app, or a countdown that nudges you to reconsider. Research shows that even a small delay significantly reduces mindless app-opening.
When you open a protected app, Friction draws an overlay on top of it. You'll see a countdown ring (the pause duration you set). After the timer ends, a slide-to-open button unlocks. If strict mode is on, you also need to choose an intention before the button unlocks.
When strict mode is enabled for an app, the slide-to-open button remains locked until you select a reason for opening it (e.g. Boredom, Work, Habit check). This adds reflection to the pause and is especially effective for social media. Choosing an intention is recorded locally so you can review your patterns later.
Network pace uses a local VPN to slow down the data connection for a specific app. Infinite-scroll feeds become less compelling when images load slowly. The VPN is entirely local — your traffic does not leave your device through any external server. You can choose Gentle, Moderate, or Aggressive throttle levels.
Android does not offer a public API to detect which app the user just opened. The Accessibility Service is the most reliable way to receive app-open events in real time. Friction only reads the app package name — it never reads screen content or keystrokes.

The VPN permission is used for the network throttle feature to apply a speed cap. The VPN runs entirely on-device. If you don't use network throttling, the VPN is never activated.
Friction runs a lightweight monitoring service in the background. On most devices this is negligible. However, some OEM battery-saver systems (Xiaomi MIUI, OnePlus, Realme, Vivo, Huawei) aggressively kill background services. If Friction stops working after a while, go to your battery settings and set Friction to 'No restrictions' or 'Unrestricted'.
Go to Settings → Data & Privacy → Clear all usage data. This permanently deletes open-event history, usage records, and resets daily open counters. Your protection rules (apps you've protected and their settings) are not affected.
